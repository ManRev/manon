Welcome!

Senior Research Scientist, Google DeepMind

PhD in Social and Engineering Systems and Statistics, MIT · Postdoctoral Fellow, Harvard · Postdoctoral Research Scientist, Meta FAIR

Interests: probabilistic modeling · multi-agent decision theory · network science · computational social choice · reinforcement learning from human feedback

Hi! I'm Manon. I research collaborative decision-making in humans. My work bridges mathematical frameworks with large-scale empirical studies to study collective agency in a networked, AI-mediated world.

During my PhD at MIT, I developed mathematical theories and led the first laboratory experiments evaluating liquid democracy—a scheme where individuals delegate decisions dynamically using second-order knowledge (what I know about what you know).

Previously, I was a Doctoral Fellow at the Harvard Kennedy School and started and led an AI and democracy research agenda at the Berkman Klein Center. I have taught at MIT and the University of Notre Dame, and interned at Palantir, the Center for Strategic and International Studies, and Bell Labs.

Projects

Improving Group Deliberation through AI-Guided Self-Reflection New

tl;dr: A brief interaction with a non-directive AI reflector prior to discussion improves group deliberation quality, outgroup empathy, and understanding.

Our work tests a non-directive AI reflector (AIR) designed to scaffold private inner reflection before group conversations. In a randomized experiment with 1,173 participants across 391 groups, individuals spent 7 minutes watching a control video, writing a reflection statement, or chatting with AIR (a prompted gemma-4-31b-it model asking non-directive questions).

AIR significantly improved group discourse quality compared to control (Cohen's d = 0.31) and solitary reflection (d = 0.27), particularly in disagreeing groups, while enhancing outgroup empathy. Solitary reflection offered no benefit over control and slightly polarized stances (d = 0.14). Total opinion change was uniform across conditions, meaning AIR shifted the timing of reconsideration rather than steering opinions.

Read the paper.

Joint work with Haiwen Li, Crystal Qian, Ben Usman, Stephanie Chan, Michiel Bakker, Srivignesh Rajendran, Avneesh Sud, Thomas Leung, Nikki Hariri, John Canny, and Nick Dufour

AI-Enhanced Deliberative Democracy and the Future of the Collective Will

tl;dr: We review mathematical methods used to elicit and approximate the collective will—from Dr Gallup's poll to DeepMind's Habermas Machine.

This article examines the technical and normative design choices behind computational frameworks for finding common ground across preferences. Tracing preference elicitation from traditional opinion polls to AI-facilitated collective judgment, we highlight its potential as a sense-making and agreement-seeking discovery tool while cautioning against dangerous uses like binding decisions or post-rationalizing political outcomes.

Joint work with Théophile Pénigaud

Recommender Systems with Justified Representation

tl;dr: Pro-social ranking algorithms can inadvertently hide legitimate viewpoints. We develop ranking algorithms that guarantee the representation of plural perspectives.

While algorithms designed to foster high-quality discussions in comment sections help filter out toxicity, they can also inadvertently suppress legitimate plural viewpoints. This paper introduces justified representation (JR) from social choice into comment-ranking algorithms. Enforcing JR guarantees greater inclusion of diverse perspectives while remaining fully compatible with optimizing for user engagement and conversational quality.

Joint work with Smitha Milli, Tyler Lu, Jamelle Watson-Daniels and Max Nickel

Value Alignment in AI: Resistance in RLHF

tl;dr: Some reward models in RLHF resist learning training split preferences, assigning higher rewards to human-rejected entries.

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) aligns language models using reward models (RMs) trained on binary preferences. SEAL: Systematic Error Analysis for Value Alignment introduces new metrics—feature imprint, alignment resistance, and alignment robustness—to evaluate how well human values are modeled. Testing open-source components, we found a 26% incidence of alignment resistance, where RMs fail to match human preferences on training data.

Joint work with Matteo Cargnelutti, Tyna Eloundou, and Greg Leppert

Liquid Democracy: Selecting Experts Democratically

tl;dr: We extend the Condorcet Jury Theorem to transitive delegation networks, proving new statistical properties and validating them with human experiments.

Liquid democracy allows voters to participate directly or delegate votes transitively to trusted peers, leveraging second-order social graph knowledge. To evaluate its likelihood of success, we developed a mathematical theory mapping local delegation behaviors to macro graph dynamics in In Defense of Liquid Democracy, proving new results for infinite Pólya-urn processes and weighted majorities. This framework was validated through human experiments (Liquid Democracy in Practice) and robustly matched theory in Tracking Truth with Liquid Democracy.

Joint work with Adam Berinsky, Daniel Halpern, Joe Halpern, Ali Jadbabaie, Elchanan Mossel and Ariel Procaccia

Congress: The Optimal Number of Representatives

tl;dr: We derive new optimal sizing and aggregation rules for representative assemblies within the Condorcet epistemic framework.

However small the Republic may be, the Representatives must be raised to a certain number... and however large it may be, they must be divided to certain number... Federalist Paper No.10, James Madison

In How Many Representatives Do We Need?, we answer what optimal number of unweighted experts maximizes majority-vote correctness. Contrary to past sub-linear growth conjectures, we prove that sampling top experts requires an optimal congress size that scales linearly with the population size, holding even under high individual accuracy. However, if competence declines too rapidly with scale, a single expert can asymptotically outperform a majority vote.

Joint work with Daniel Halpern and Tao Lin
